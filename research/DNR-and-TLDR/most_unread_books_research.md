# The Most Unread Books: Data, Methodologies, and Rankings

## 1. Datasets and Methodologies

There is no official "most abandoned" metric provided directly by Goodreads because the platform doesn't have a native "Did Not Finish" (DNF) button. However, researchers and data enthusiasts have used several methods to infer this data.

### Method A: User-Generated Shelves (Goodreads Data)
Researchers often parse datasets that include user tags/shelves. They look for custom shelves named "DNF," "abandoned," "did-not-finish," or "unfinished."
*   **The Data:** Datasets like the *Goodbooks-10k* (a popular dataset of 10,000 books used for recommender systems) or the *UCSD Goodreads Datasets* (which includes millions of user-book interactions) are often mined for this.
*   **The Caveat (Popularity Bias):** This method inherently favors incredibly popular books. A book read by 10 million people with a 1% abandonment rate will have more "DNF" tags (100,000) than a book read by 10,000 people with a 50% abandonment rate (5,000). Thus, the "most abandoned" lists on Goodreads are usually just lists of bestsellers that weren't for everyone.

### Method B: "Want to Read" vs. "Read" Gap
Another method is to look at the gap between the number of people who shelved a book as "Want to Read" or "Currently Reading" versus those who eventually marked it as "Read." 

### Method C: The Hawking Index (Amazon Kindle Data)
In 2014, mathematician Jordan Ellenberg created the satirical **Hawking Index** to estimate how much of a book people actually finish.
*   **How it works:** He looked at Amazon Kindle's "Popular Highlights" feature. He took the average page number of the five most highlighted passages in a book and divided it by the total page count. A lower percentage implies that readers stopped highlighting early on, suggesting they abandoned the book.
*   **The namesake:** The index is named after Stephen Hawking’s *A Brief History of Time*, widely joked about as the "most unread book of all time."
